# A1 – Creating a Portfolio

## Objective - Analyze Portfolio's and use the Analyze, Decide, and Communicate Pillars for a product
Before we analyze any portfolio's, we should be sure of what we're looking for. The criteria are as follows: Navigability (Can I find things in the portfolio/website?), Reproducibility (Can someone look at the portfolio and reproduce something similar?), Evidence of Reasoning (Is there a final answer? How was it determined?), and Professional Tone. The first portfolio I analyzed was on Github, (here it is listed in case you wish to view it: https://ejhoerl.github.io/). Going through this portfolio, it was navigable with ease even to me, a new user to Github. It was obviously very creative and even a new user such as myself could scroll through it and find the information it presented to me with just a click or two. This portfolio does present some projects this man has worked on and I can say there is plenty of evidence of reasoning for many of his various projects, some being coding, some being paperwork/calculations on certain projects, and even contributing to the design of the project itself with a clear explanation of the goals and path to the final results. Lastly, this portfolio is indeed professional and creative.

The next portfolio I analyzed was located here: https://tylerwisniewski.github.io/. As I clicked through this portfolio, I found it easily navigable with some of the navigations being explained simply, such as "Click on Image to learn more about this project" which aids with first time users who might not know how to navigate different tabs or projects. This portfolio is not only professional, but creative to a degree that isn't too far for business and not too bland. The projects this man has worked on are very impressive and I applaud the fact that he even includes final reports after simple summaries of his projects, goals during the project, and final results. An exceptionally well-done Portfolio.
