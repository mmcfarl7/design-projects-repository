I am an undergraduate at UNC Charlotte. My concentration is Mechanical Engineering, but I would like to focus on Additive Manufacturing. I love fishing, nature, flowers, gaming, books, and writing. I am very well versed in Office 365. I am very adaptive and can solve almost any problem with one of many imagined solutions.

I have worked on a few small-scale projects from various classes, but I think the one that always stuck was from one intro-level engineering class where me and a team of 3 other people were tasked with using paper to create a tower that met a certain height limit. We had fake money for a budget, guidelines to follow as if we were making a real blueprint, and even a warning of a heavy fine if we built under the requested limit and another heavy fine if the tower fell over in a "Wind Test" against his classroom fan. It was a big competition against 5 other teams and every detail counted. Even scotch tape and cutting the paper meant spending some of your budget. Everyone tried their absolute hardest to try and save the most money while having the highest tower that by the end my table placed 2nd, which we were proud of. We had designed the tower with folds and bends instead of cuts and tape, so through origami we saved almost as much as the winning team who used geometry to win. After class, I talked to the professor about what the best answer was. He smiled, took a singular paper and crumbled it into a ball and placed it on the table with a single piece of tape. He explained that everyone tried brainstorming such elaborate ideas that even the most ridiculous answer passed. The paper ball broke the heavy fine of being too short, but it passed the wind test so by technicality by using near to nothing he saved the most money than some elaborate tower system like everyone designed. I think the morale of that story will forever be with me that not all solutions require a brainstorm, just basic analysis and a step back to understanding the priorities.
